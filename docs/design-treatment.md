## Game Concept

<!-- Explain why the working title fits. Mention alternative titles if useful. -->
At the moment, the working title is "Temple Snag". The previous working title was "Finders Keepers". Both titles are apt because the game entails the players working their way through the board in order to reach an item of interest first. Once they have the item in their possession, their goal is to keep it away from the other players for as long as possible. For the other players who do not have it in their possession, their objective is to "snag" it from the one holding it. The new title is more fitting for the game because it fits the theme of the game: all the players are tomb raiders who are aiming to be the first to grab a precious artifact, and "snag" it from the one who has it if they themselves don't.

<!-- Describe the setting, theme, and story or premise. Identify the characters
or the role the player takes. A game without a story can describe its situation
and player roles instead; do not invent a narrative just to fill this section. -->
Parts of the game were inspired by Monopoly. The idea was "What if Monopoly was reversed, and had the premise of the old game Keep Away?" Except, rather than having the game's theme being a simple "you're a thief protecting their loot," instead you are a tomb raider working towards the location of an artifact that you must snag before the other players do (as stated previously). The main goal of each player is to escape the temple before the others by holding onto the artifact for as long as you can.

## Gameplay

<!-- Explain the player's objectives, the conflicts or challenges, and how play
succeeds, fails, or ends. Describe the recurring decisions and actions, the rules
that connect them, and what changes in the game as a result. Game state means the
current situation, such as positions, resources, score, or available choices.
Describe the intended experience through those interactions. -->
The main goal of the player, as stated in the previous section, is to reach the artifact before the other players are able to. Once a player reaches the artifact and snags it, their new goal is to hold onto the artifact for as long as possible. The other players' new goal is to reach the player holding the artifact as fast as they can and snag the it from them. Once they have it, the cycle repeats. Each time every player has a turn, the player holding the artifact gains a point. The player who reaches the specified amount of points is the winner. The challenge that is posed against the player holding the artifact is that they have to remain ahead of all the other players as much as they can. Otherwise, they will lose the piece. For the ones not holding the artifact, their challenge is reaching the player holding it. This is especially difficult for those who consistently roll low whenever it is their turn to move. 

The core loop of the game involves the player rolling a pair of dice in order to move along the spaces on the board. The board itself is a looping board, like Monopoly as an example. The main factor that influences the player's decision is whether or not they are holding the artifact. Do they move with the intent to catch up with the one holding it, or do they instead aim for spaces on the board that would pose hinderances to them instead. If they are holding it, do they move ahead as fast as possible, or do they sabotage their competitors instead so they are able to hold onto it for as long as possible? Distance between each player on the board is also a deciding factor. Players who are far behind may opt to slow down their opponents, or instead brute force their way to the front with high rolls. For the former, it may benefit them in the long run, and the latter it may work but with potentially more drawbacks. 

### Setup And Start

<!-- Describe the starting state: how the play area and pieces are arranged,
what each player begins with, and how play starts. Refer to the pieces described
below rather than listing them twice. -->
The game begins with every player rolling a dice. The player who rolls the highest is the one to move first. Once that is decided, that player will then roll both die in order to determine how face the artifact is away from the starting position. To finish this off, the player who rolled the lowest will roll both die as well, and the two will be added together. That sum will be the location on the board where the artifact will sit. If there are two players who roll the same number, the two will roll against each other to break the tie. Before any player starts rolling their dice to move, they all decide on how many points a player should score in order to win. The default is 10 points, with the maximum being 21. All players begin on the same space on the board to give each player equal opportunity to reach the artifact, with the only thing changing that being the opening dice roll. 

### Core Gameplay Loop

<!-- Describe the recurring player choices and actions, the rules that connect
them, and what changes as a result. Game state is the current situation, such as
positions, resources, score, or available choices. -->
As previously stated, the core gameplay loop revolves around players rolling a pair of die and snagging an artifact that is laid out somewhere on the board. Things for the player to consider include spacing between themself and other players, whether or not they are currently holding the artifact, how many points each player has, and what spaces are in front of them that will allow them to fast track their own progress. If they have the artifact, the question they ask themself turns into "how do I stay ahead of everyone else now?" If they don't, the question becomes "how do I get ahead of the artifact holder, then how do I stay ahead of everyone else?" If the player holding the artifact almost has enough points to win, then the other players need to devise ways to get the artifact away from them. 

### Ending And Outcome

<!-- State when play ends and how the outcome is decided. Include win/lose
conditions where applicable, or another clear beginning/end structure. -->
The game ends when a single player reaches the required amount of points. After each player has made their move, that marks the end of a turn. At the end of a turn, the player holding the artifact gains a point. When a player has gained enough points (with the point goal having been established at the beginning of the game), then that player is the winner. In the context of the game, this means that the tomb raider has escaped with the artifact before the other players have. Once a player wins, the game ends, and the other players lose. 

## First Physical Prototype

<!-- Describe the planned physical play area and its pieces or materials, such as
boards, cards, tokens, dice, or a score sheet. Use only what this game needs.
Explain what the pieces represent and how a player uses them to try the core
loop. This is a feasible plan, not a finished prototype or playtest result. -->
The planned prototype will consist of five main things: the board itself for the boardgame, a pair of die to roll, the pieces for the players, cards, and the artifact that they will try to snag and hold onto. The board will have spaces arranged all around its edges, similar to monopoly, with each space having a different type of effect denoted by a symbol. The cards will be the same, and will be acquired through certain spaces that grant them. There will be a card zone in the center of the board for the players to draw from. Each player will only be able to hold 2 cards at a time, and must return a card to the deck when they acquire a third. The dice are self explanatory, as they are the players' method of movement. The board for the board game is feasible enough, I would only need cardboard. There were dice that I could purchase in the NJIT bookstore, so that will be no issue either. The other three will be trickier to acquire. Cards alone will be difficult to print as much as I need, and player pieces will either have to be salvaged from other sources or hand-made. The same goes for the artifact that the players need to hold on to. 
<!-- Describe the planned or current physical play area. Mark pieces that have
not been made as planned, and update this section as the prototype develops.

The plan has changed since then, with the conceptualized prototype being a little more simplified, but not by much and in different places it has also increased in complexity. The board itself is shaped as a cross, that way it can remain relatively small but still have just as much spaces for the players to travel on. I've done away with the cards so there's no need to worry about printing, instead opting for just the spaces on the board that will offer benefits and/or drawbacks for players that land on them. If possible, I'd like to add a spinner to the center of the board as well to replace the cards but that is not a necessity. Player pieces and artifacts still must be made at this time.

### Materials And Mechanic Adjustments

<!-- Explain which materials are created, adapted, or reused and why they work
for this prototype. Describe how rules, values, layouts, or pieces can be changed
between playthroughs without rebuilding the whole prototype. Focus on usable,
readable materials and actual design choices. -->
Created materials include the board for the board game itself, player pieces, and the artifact piece. As for any reused pieces, the only thing that would qualify would be the dice used by the players to move. These materials would work for this prototype given that they fulfill all the core requirements for play to function properly in this game. A rule that could be subject to change without the need to rebuild the whole prototype would be the point requirement to win. Changing how much a player needs to score simple makes the game either longer or shorter. Another rule that could be changed is whether or not players work together. The core game mechanics stay the same, only now more than one player are working toward the same goal together, not separately. Layout of the spaces on the board wouldn't change since they are set, but the starting position of the players is possibly subject to change, so long as all players begin on the same space. 

## Intended Players

<!-- Identify the intended audience's relevant interests and play habits.
Explain why this game's decisions, theme, or intended experience would appeal
to those players. Connect the audience to specific design choices. -->
The target audience are those who enjoy complexity in simplicity. The game itself is simple on the surface, but when playing the game the players can find that there are opportunities to strategize what their next move will be when considering the different factors in play. How many points each player has, how far back or ahead they are in relation to other players, who currently has the artifact. It may also be intriguing to players who enjoy games such as Tomb Raider or series like Indiana Jones, considering the theme. Being able to play as a character in that same profession may be exciting for those who enjoy the genre. 

## Accessibility And Inclusivity

<!-- Explain concrete ways people with different abilities can perceive,
understand, and use the game's actual interactions. Explain how these choices
preserve the intended gameplay and can carry into the later digital version.
Choose considerations relevant to this game; a generic feature list is not enough. -->
The only interactions the game has at a base level is a few things: rolling the dice, moving their player piece, and dealing with the resulting effect of the space you landed on. Players with differing abilities will face the same challenges as others. Players who put more thought into the game's interactions with the players may give more thought into who their actions affect in the long run. And because the base interactions are so simple, they will translate into the digital version easily. The decisions made by the player all begin with the roll of a dice, and once they finish moving their next interaction will be determined by whatever space they land on. It may be a benefit, drawback, or nothing at all. For that reason play is preserved regardless of who the player is and what abilities they may have. 

## Unique Selling Points (USP)

<!-- Unique selling points are the distinctive design choices that give intended
players a reason to choose this game. Explain those choices through a relevant
comparison with an existing game. Connect the differences to the intended
players' experience; support the comparison with concrete design details. -->
The unique selling point of the game is that it is essentially an inverse of its inspiration, with that inspiration being Monopoly with bits of the game Keep-Away. In Monopoly, your main goal is to play until you have bought every purchasable space on the board. In this game, your goal is to steal exactly one artifact before anyone else does. In addition to this, there is the challenge of keeping the artifact away from the other players for as long as you can. The more proficient you are at it, the faster you win. On the other side, players who are the most efficient with moving on the board will also be able to snag the artifact quicker, and subsequently win faster. The USP in that sense is the question "are you skilled enough to catch up, and keep ahead?"

## Concept Pitch

<!-- Bring together the concept, theme, intended audience, player experience,
and strongest USP in a concise pitch. Summarize the earlier sections rather
than repeating their full details. -->
Temple Snag is a multiplayer dice-rolling board game of both strategy and chance. You and 3 other players are tomb raiders whose goal is to reach the ancient artifact located somewhere on the board. Roll high on the dice to make it there faster, and do your best to keep the artifact with you. If you don't have it, then you better roll higher and snag it from the one who does. Be wary of the spaces you land on, too. Some spaces are traps, either for you or your opponents. Are you fast enough to reach the artifact before your competitors do? And are you skilled enough to stay ahead of them long enough to keep it? Or will your opponents get the better of you and snag it before you can claim victory? Good luck, and careful where you step in the temple!
