# IT265 Module 2: One-Page Treatment

**Working game title:** Finders Keepers

Write one coherent, one-page-length treatment. Use the four prompts to begin, then combine your answers into natural prose.

## Four-Sentence Core

1. **Premise:** Who is the player, what is the situation, and what problem matters?
2. **Experience and loop:** What should play feel like, and what does the player repeatedly decide or do?
3. **Goal and pressure:** What changes, what limits the player, and how does play end?
4. **Scope:** What makes this version distinctive, and what can you test first with affordable physical materials?

## Treatment Draft

In this game concept "Finders Keepers", the players act as bandits of sorts, all of whom are aiming to take possession of a piece that will grant them victory, so long as they manage to hold onto it. The only problem that matters is whether or not you have possession of this victory item. Those who don't have the item will feel a sense of urgency to do what it takes to get it for themselves. The one holding it will feel the urgency to find ways to prolong holding onto it themself. 

As stated previously, the item itself is what grants victory. Holding the item at the end of every turn grants one point to the one holding it. The first player who reaches 21 points wins the game. In this sense, the only thing that truly changes is the holder of the item. Holding the item also presents limitations for it's current holder as well. The most that player should be able to do is to avoid and evade other players, unless they are suddenly unable to move. The game itself ends when someone is able to reach 21 points before the others do, and that player is the winner.

The game features a specificity in that it is essentially the inverse of Hot-Potato. Rather than the player doing their best to not end up with the item being passed around, the player instead will seek to prevent the other player or players from taking the item from them. In addition to this, players not holding the item will have the option to either skip ahead and grab the piece ahead of time, or even prevent the player who has the piece from moving via either special spaces or, later on, cards. However, for a physical prototype with affordable materials, what would be tested would be the base game itself. That is, just the special spaces on the board itself, the player pieces, special item, and a dice for the players to make moves.
